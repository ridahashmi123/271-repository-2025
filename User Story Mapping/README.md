Why should we use User Story Mapping?

✅ Understand the User Journey – Helps teams see how users interact with the product step by step.

✅ Prioritize Features – Focuses on delivering the most valuable features first.

✅ Align Teams – Keeps developers, designers, and stakeholders on the same page.

✅ Break Down Complexity – Organizes tasks into manageable steps for agile development.


A USM is also an "artifact", which is any physical (or virtual) object created in the process of aiding software development. This includes User Story Maps, Kanban boards, UML models, Requirements Documents.

Brief Explanation:

Initially, I brainstormed 4 main goals of a delivery app (the epics) on paper to get a rough layout of how I wanted to 
organize the app and deducted the specific tasks that I would need from there. I looked at food delivery apps like DoorDash 
and UberEats for inspiration which drove me to include tasks like receiving delivery status updates, including the live map,
filtering products by different categories, and more. For coupon/tracking points ideas I talked to my mom about her in-store experiences
with points collection and the different types. After that, the brainstorm looked messy because there seemed to be too many tasks to 
1 goal, so I split the main 4 epics into swimlane categories if necessary to further categorize the tasks effectively and cleanly. 
This final submission actually ended up being a remake of my first attempt at this assignment on StoriesOnBoard. Overall, in the 
brainstorm I went from epics to stories to tasks, and then I later added or refined the stories and swimlanes depending on how I could 
further categorize the tasks, leading me to include more tasks that I thought were necessary to make sense of the added swimlanes.

Three general decisions I made while creating the storymap included the 4 main goals I wanted to achieve of user registration, 
browing/searching, order management, and delivery/feedback. Then, as I was creating tasks, I had in mind which ones would make
sense as MVPs and those were mostly the ones I wrote down first as I could imagine them in an initial test launch and because
the preceding tasks developed off of them. Thirdly, I chose statuses to make it seem as if the first testing version of the app was
almost finished and ready to launch for feedback, so most of the MVPs are marked as done/ready with only a few "doing" statuses, whereas
the tasks schedules for the second release relate more to addditional feedback methods and are mostly marked as "doing". This
third step is relevant to the different personas I assigned for the "doing" tasks because I had 2 front-end developers, 3 back-end 
developers, and one integration tester and assigned them according to the tasks and their statuses.

The results of these decisions are captured in the visual assignment of the personas in the Discovery screen. Additionally,
regarding statuses, the MVPs are shown to be mostly done, while the tasks scheduled for the second release are mostly in progress,
and the unscheduled tasks are still not started on because they have a lower priority than the MVP and secondary launch tasks.

The extra feature I tried was assigning personas to the tasks.
